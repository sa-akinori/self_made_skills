# Detail Levels for Report Structures

This document defines the five detail levels for report structure generation.

---

## Level 1: Minimal (Chapter Titles Only)

**Use when**: User needs a quick overview or wants maximum flexibility in defining content.

**Includes**:
- Chapter numbers and titles only
- No subsections
- No descriptions

**Example**:
```markdown
# Report Title

## Chapter 1: Executive Summary
## Chapter 2: Background and Objectives
## Chapter 3: Technical Analysis
## Chapter 4: Market Analysis
## Chapter 5: Conclusions and Recommendations
## Appendices
```

**Typical length**: 10-20 lines

---

## Level 2: Concise (Chapters with Subsections)

**Use when**: User needs a structured outline without detailed explanations.

**Includes**:
- Chapter titles
- Major subsections (2-4 per chapter)
- No descriptions or explanations

**Example**:
```markdown
# Report Title

## Chapter 1: Executive Summary
- Key findings
- Recommendations

## Chapter 2: Background and Objectives
- Research context
- Objectives and scope

## Chapter 3: Technical Analysis
- Current approaches
- Challenges and limitations
- Emerging solutions

## Chapter 4: Conclusions
- Summary of findings
- Next steps
```

**Typical length**: 30-50 lines

---

## Level 3: Standard (Chapters with Descriptions)

**Use when**: User needs clear guidance on what each section should cover.

**Includes**:
- Chapter titles
- Subsections (2-5 per chapter)
- Brief description of each subsection's content
- Key questions to address

**Example**:
```markdown
# Report Title

## Chapter 1: Executive Summary (2-3 pages)

**Purpose**: Provide a concise overview for decision-makers

### 1.1 Key Findings
- Summarize the most important discoveries
- Highlight critical insights

### 1.2 Recommendations
- Present actionable recommendations
- Prioritize by impact and feasibility

## Chapter 2: Background and Objectives (3-5 pages)

**Purpose**: Establish context and define research scope

### 2.1 Research Context
- Explain the background and motivation
- Describe the current situation

### 2.2 Objectives and Scope
- Define specific research objectives
- Clarify what is included and excluded
```

**Typical length**: 80-120 lines

---

## Level 4: Detailed (With Content Guidance and Suggestions)

**Use when**: User needs comprehensive guidance on content and structure.

**Includes**:
- Chapter titles with page estimates
- Detailed subsections (3-6 per chapter)
- Content descriptions for each subsection
- Suggested figures, tables, or data to include
- Key questions and analysis points

**Example**:
```markdown
# Report Title

## Chapter 1: Executive Summary (2-3 pages)

**Purpose**: Provide a standalone overview that enables decision-making without reading the full report

### 1.1 Research Background
- Briefly explain the research motivation
- Identify the key problem or opportunity
- State why this research is important now

### 1.2 Key Findings
Present the 3-5 most important discoveries:
- Finding 1: [Description]
- Finding 2: [Description]
- Use bullet points for clarity

**Suggested content**:
- Summary table of key findings
- One-page visual summary (infographic or flowchart)

### 1.3 Strategic Recommendations
Provide actionable recommendations:
- Short-term actions (1-6 months)
- Medium-term initiatives (6-18 months)
- Long-term strategy (18+ months)

**Analysis points**:
- Prioritize by impact and feasibility
- Include resource requirements
- Identify quick wins

## Chapter 2: Background and Objectives (3-5 pages)

### 2.1 Industry/Domain Context
- Current state of the field
- Historical developments
- Key players and stakeholders

**Suggested figures**:
- Timeline of key developments
- Market landscape diagram

### 2.2 Problem Statement
- Detailed description of the challenge or opportunity
- Why existing solutions are inadequate
- Stakeholder perspectives

### 2.3 Research Objectives
Define specific, measurable objectives:
- Primary objectives
- Secondary objectives
- Success criteria

**Key questions to address**:
- What specific questions will this research answer?
- What decisions will this research inform?
```

**Typical length**: 150-250 lines

---

## Level 5: Comprehensive (Maximum Detail)

**Use when**: User needs a complete, ready-to-execute report framework with extensive guidance.

**Includes**:
- All elements from Level 4
- Detailed subsection breakdowns (4-8 items per chapter)
- Comprehensive content descriptions
- Multiple suggestions for figures, tables, and data sources
- Detailed analysis frameworks
- Cross-references between chapters
- Methodological guidance
- Data collection recommendations

**Example**:
```markdown
# Report Title

## Front Matter

### Cover Page
- Report title
- Subtitle (if applicable)
- Date of publication
- Author(s)

### Table of Contents
- Auto-generated from chapter structure
- Include page numbers

### List of Figures
- Figure 1: [Description]
- Figure 2: [Description]

### List of Tables
- Table 1: [Description]
- Table 2: [Description]

---

## Chapter 1: Executive Summary (2-3 pages)

**Purpose**: Provide a standalone overview that enables decision-making without reading the full report. This chapter should be written last, after all other content is complete.

**Target audience**: Executives, decision-makers, and stakeholders who may only read this section

### 1.1 Research Background and Motivation

**Content to include**:
- The business or scientific context that motivated this research
- The key problem, opportunity, or knowledge gap
- Why this research is timely and important
- Brief mention of stakeholders affected

**Writing guidelines**:
- Keep to 1-2 paragraphs
- Avoid jargon; use accessible language
- Establish urgency and relevance

### 1.2 Research Scope and Methodology

**Content to include**:
- What was investigated (and what was not)
- High-level description of research methods used
- Data sources and timeframe
- Any important limitations or constraints

**Suggested format**:
- Short bulleted list of research activities
- One sentence summary of methodology

### 1.3 Key Findings

Present the 3-5 most important discoveries in order of significance:

**For each finding**:
- Clear statement of the finding
- Supporting evidence (1-2 key data points)
- Implication or "so what"

**Suggested visualizations**:
- Summary table: Key findings with implications
- Infographic: One-page visual summary
- Comparison chart: Before/after or current/proposed

**Writing guidelines**:
- Use active voice
- Lead with the conclusion, not the method
- Quantify whenever possible

### 1.4 Strategic Recommendations

Provide actionable recommendations prioritized by timeline and impact:

**Short-term actions (1-6 months)**:
- List 2-3 immediate steps
- Include resource requirements
- Identify quick wins

**Medium-term initiatives (6-18 months)**:
- List 3-4 strategic projects
- Describe expected outcomes
- Estimate resource needs

**Long-term strategy (18+ months)**:
- Outline 1-2 transformational initiatives
- Describe vision and end state
- Acknowledge dependencies

**Suggested table**:
| Recommendation | Timeline | Impact | Effort | Priority |
|---------------|----------|---------|--------|----------|
| Rec 1         | Short    | High    | Low    | 1        |
| Rec 2         | Medium   | High    | Medium | 2        |

**Analysis framework**:
- Use impact/effort matrix for prioritization
- Consider risks and dependencies
- Align with organizational capabilities

### 1.5 Next Steps

**Content to include**:
- Immediate actions required
- Decision points
- Resources needed
- Proposed timeline for implementation

---

## Chapter 2: Background and Objectives (3-5 pages)

**Purpose**: Establish comprehensive context and clearly define the research scope. This chapter should provide all necessary background for readers unfamiliar with the topic.

### 2.1 Domain/Industry Overview

**Content to include**:
- Overview of the relevant field, industry, or domain
- Current state and recent developments
- Key terminology and concepts (consider a glossary box)

**Analysis points**:
- What is the current state of the field?
- What recent changes or trends are relevant?
- Who are the key players and stakeholders?

**Suggested figures**:
- Industry landscape diagram
- Timeline of key developments
- Ecosystem map showing relationships

**Data sources to reference**:
- Industry reports
- Market research
- Academic literature
- Trade publications

### 2.2 Historical Context and Evolution

**Content to include**:
- How the current situation developed
- Key milestones and inflection points
- Previous attempts to address similar problems
- Lessons learned from past approaches

**Suggested visualization**:
- Timeline chart showing evolution
- Comparison: then vs. now

### 2.3 Problem Statement and Opportunity

**Content to include**:
- Detailed description of the problem or opportunity
- Who is affected and how
- Why existing solutions are inadequate
- The cost or impact of inaction
- The potential value of solving this problem

**Framework to use**:
- Problem trees or root cause analysis
- Stakeholder impact assessment
- Gap analysis

**Key questions to address**:
- What exactly is the problem?
- Why does it matter?
- What are the consequences if not addressed?
- What would success look like?

### 2.4 Research Objectives and Questions

**Primary objectives**:
List 1-3 main objectives:
- Objective 1: [Specific, measurable goal]
- Objective 2: [Specific, measurable goal]

**Secondary objectives**:
List 2-4 supporting objectives:
- Support objective 1: [Description]
- Support objective 2: [Description]

**Research questions**:
For each objective, list specific questions to answer:
- Question 1.1: [Detailed question]
- Question 1.2: [Detailed question]

**Success criteria**:
Define how success will be measured:
- Criterion 1: [Measurable indicator]
- Criterion 2: [Measurable indicator]

### 2.5 Scope and Boundaries

**In scope**:
- Clearly define what is included in the research
- Specify geographic, temporal, or other boundaries
- List included topics, technologies, or approaches

**Out of scope**:
- Explicitly state what is excluded
- Explain why certain areas were excluded
- Acknowledge limitations

**Assumptions and constraints**:
- List key assumptions made
- Identify resource or access constraints
- Note any dependencies

### 2.6 Research Methodology Overview

**Content to include**:
- Overview of research approach
- Data collection methods
- Analysis frameworks used
- Quality assurance measures

**Note**: Detailed methodology may appear in a dedicated chapter for complex research. This section provides orientation for readers.
```

## Selecting the Appropriate Detail Level

**Level 1**: Quick outline, maximum flexibility needed
**Level 2**: Need structure but want to define own content
**Level 3**: Want clear guidance on what to include (most common)
**Level 4**: Need comprehensive planning with suggestions
**Level 5**: Want a complete, executable framework (for large or complex reports)

**Default recommendation**: Level 3 for most users, adjust based on:
- Report complexity
- User's familiarity with the topic
- Time available for planning
- Team collaboration needs
