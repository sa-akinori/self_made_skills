## Scientific & Technical Research Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Objective and impartial**: Avoid subjective language
- **Formal and academic**: Use precise technical terminology
- **Data-driven**: Emphasize quantitative evidence
- **Methodical**: Clearly explain methodologies and rationale

### Language Characteristics
- **Passive voice acceptable** for methodology sections (e.g., "Data was collected...")
- **Active voice preferred** for results and discussion (e.g., "The results demonstrate...")
- **Precise technical terminology**: Use domain-specific terms accurately
- **Hedging language** when appropriate (e.g., "suggests", "indicates", "appears to")

### Structure
- **Logical flow**: Background → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusions
- **Subsections clearly delineated** with descriptive headings
- **Heavy use of citations**: Every claim supported by references
- **Figures and tables integrated** with detailed captions
- **Methodology transparency**: Sufficient detail for reproducibility

### Sentence Style
- **Moderate to complex sentences**: Can use subordinate clauses for precision
- **Average length**: 20-30 words
- **Avoid contractions**: Use "cannot" not "can't"
- **Third person**: Generally avoid "I" or "we" except in methodology

### Example Phrases
- "The data suggest that..."
- "Previous studies have demonstrated..."
- "To investigate this phenomenon, we employed..."
- "The results indicate a statistically significant correlation..."
- "These findings have important implications for..."

### Citation Style
- **In-text**: Numbered citations [1] or author-year (Smith et al., 2023)
- **Reference format**: Follow scientific journal standards (Nature, Science format)

## Business Strategy Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Pragmatic and action-oriented**: Focus on implementation
- **Confident but measured**: Avoid overstatement
- **Business-focused**: Emphasize ROI, competitive advantage, market impact
- **Persuasive**: Build a case for recommendations

### Language Characteristics
- **Active voice strongly preferred**: "We recommend..." not "It is recommended..."
- **Executive-friendly**: Minimize jargon, explain technical terms
- **Concrete and specific**: Use numbers, timelines, deliverables
- **Forward-looking**: Emphasize future opportunities and risks

### Structure
- **Executive summary first**: Key findings and recommendations upfront
- **Clear recommendations**: Actionable, prioritized, with rationale
- **Risk/benefit analysis**: Balanced view of options
- **Implementation roadmap**: Practical steps, timelines, resources

### Sentence Style
- **Short to moderate sentences**: 15-25 words average
- **Bulleted lists**: For options, recommendations, action items
- **Direct statements**: Avoid excessive hedging
- **Second person acceptable**: "You should consider..."

### Example Phrases
- "We recommend pursuing Strategy A because..."
- "This approach offers three key advantages..."
- "The primary risk is... which can be mitigated by..."
- "Based on our analysis, the optimal path forward is..."
- "Immediate action items include..."

### Visual Elements
- **Strategic frameworks**: SWOT, Porter's Five Forces, Value Chain
- **Roadmaps and timelines**: Gantt charts, milestone diagrams
- **Financial projections**: Tables, trend graphs

## Market Research Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Analytical and insights-driven**: Uncover patterns and trends
- **Objective but interpretive**: Data plus meaning
- **Market-focused**: Emphasize customer, competition, trends
- **Predictive**: Forward-looking implications

### Language Characteristics
- **Active voice for insights**: "The data reveal..."
- **Quantitative emphasis**: Numbers, percentages, statistics prominent
- **Trend language**: "Growing", "declining", "emerging", "shifting"
- **Comparative**: Benchmarking, year-over-year, segment comparisons

### Structure
- **Data-heavy sections**: Market size, segmentation, trends
- **Customer insights**: Behavior, preferences, pain points
- **Competitive landscape**: Market share, positioning
- **Forecasts**: Growth projections, scenario planning

### Sentence Style
- **Short sentences for key insights**: 12-20 words
- **Data integrated into prose**: "Market size reached $X billion, representing Y% growth"
- **Clear topic sentences**: Each paragraph has a clear point

### Example Phrases
- "Market analysis reveals..."
- "Customer surveys indicate that 65% of respondents..."
- "Year-over-year growth of 12% suggests..."
- "Three key trends are shaping the market..."
- "We project the market will reach... by 2030"

### Visual Elements
- **Charts and graphs**: Bar charts, line graphs, pie charts
- **Market segmentation diagrams**: Customer segments, demographic breakdowns
- **Competitive positioning maps**: Perceptual maps, quadrant charts

## Competitive Analysis Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Comparative and evaluative**: Weigh strengths and weaknesses
- **Strategic**: Focus on competitive positioning and implications
- **Factual but insightful**: Beyond features to strategic meaning
- **Balanced**: Fair assessment of all players

### Language Characteristics
- **Comparative language**: "Stronger than", "ahead of", "lags behind"
- **Evaluative terms**: "Advantage", "weakness", "opportunity", "threat"
- **Specific metrics**: Market share percentages, feature comparisons
- **Strategic vocabulary**: "Positioning", "differentiation", "competitive moat"

### Structure
- **Individual competitor profiles**: Detailed analysis of each player
- **Comparative matrices**: Side-by-side feature/pricing/performance tables
- **Strategic implications**: What this means for our position
- **SWOT for each competitor**: Structured assessment

### Sentence Style
- **Declarative statements**: Clear competitive assessments
- **Balanced pairs**: Acknowledge both strengths and weaknesses
- **Moderate length**: 18-25 words

### Example Phrases
- "Competitor A leads in market share but lags in innovation..."
- "The competitive landscape is characterized by..."
- "Our analysis reveals three distinct strategic groups..."
- "Company X's strength in Y is offset by weakness in Z..."
- "This competitive dynamic creates an opportunity to..."

### Visual Elements
- **Competitive matrices**: Feature comparison tables
- **Market share visualizations**: Pie charts, bar charts
- **Positioning maps**: Two-dimensional competitive space
- **Timeline comparisons**: Product launches, funding rounds

## Technology Evaluation Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Technical but accessible**: Balance depth and clarity
- **Evaluative and criteria-driven**: Systematic assessment
- **Practical**: Focus on real-world performance and fit
- **Neutral**: Unbiased assessment of options

### Language Characteristics
- **Technical precision**: Accurate use of technical specifications
- **Criteria-based language**: "Meets requirements", "exceeds benchmark"
- **Quantitative metrics**: Performance numbers, scores, ratings
- **Conditional language**: "If X, then Y" scenarios

### Structure
- **Evaluation criteria upfront**: Define what matters
- **Systematic assessment**: Each technology against same criteria
- **Scoring matrices**: Weighted evaluation tables
- **Recommendation with rationale**: Clear winner or trade-off discussion

### Sentence Style
- **Structured comparisons**: Parallel construction for fairness
- **Moderate complexity**: 18-26 words
- **Lists and tables**: Organize technical specifications

### Example Phrases
- "Against the evaluation criteria, Technology A scores..."
- "Performance benchmarks reveal that..."
- "Integration requirements include..."
- "Based on weighted scoring, we recommend..."
- "Trade-offs between options are..."

### Visual Elements
- **Evaluation matrices**: Criteria × Technologies scoring tables
- **Performance benchmarks**: Bar charts, radar charts
- **Architecture diagrams**: Technical integration views
- **Decision trees**: Selection logic flowcharts

## Product Development Research Report

### Tone & Voice
- **User-centered**: Emphasize customer needs and insights
- **Innovative but pragmatic**: Balance vision and feasibility
- **Collaborative**: "We" language, team-oriented
- **Developmental**: Progressive refinement of ideas

### Language Characteristics
- **User language**: "Users need...", "customers want..."
- **Feature-focused**: Describe capabilities and benefits
- **Feasibility terms**: "Viable", "achievable", "practical"
- **Iterative language**: "Prototype", "test", "refine", "validate"

### Structure
- **User research first**: Needs, pain points, behaviors
- **Product requirements**: Features, priorities, must-haves
- **Technical feasibility**: Can we build it?
- **Business case**: Should we build it?
- **Development roadmap**: How and when to build it

### Sentence Style
- **User story format**: "As a user, I need to..."
- **Clear feature descriptions**: What it does, why it matters
- **Short to moderate**: 15-22 words

### Example Phrases
- "User research reveals that..."
- "Key features include..."
- "This capability addresses the user need for..."
- "Technical feasibility assessment shows..."
- "The development roadmap proposes..."

### Visual Elements
- **User journey maps**: Step-by-step user experience
- **Feature prioritization matrices**: Impact vs. effort
- **Wireframes/mockups**: Visual product concepts
- **Roadmap timelines**: Phased development plan

## Custom/General Research Report

### Tone & Voice
- **Adaptable**: Match the content and audience
- **Clear and organized**: Compensate for diverse topics
- **Balanced**: Neither too technical nor too simplified
- **Transparent**: Acknowledge limitations and scope

### Language Characteristics
- **Define terms**: Don't assume shared vocabulary
- **Mixed voice**: Use what's appropriate for each section
- **Transitional phrases**: Help readers navigate diverse content
- **Explanatory**: Provide context and background

### Structure
- **Flexible organization**: By theme, by question, by stakeholder
- **Clear signposting**: Headings, summaries, transitions
- **Self-contained sections**: Each section understandable independently
- **Explicit scope**: State what is and isn't covered

### Sentence Style
- **Varied but clear**: Adjust to content complexity
- **Average 18-24 words**
- **Topic sentences**: Each section clearly introduced

### Example Phrases
- "This section examines..."
- "To understand this topic, it's important to..."
- "The following analysis addresses..."
- "Key considerations include..."
- "In summary, the research indicates..."

### Visual Elements
- **Varied as needed**: Choose visuals that clarify content
- **Conceptual diagrams**: Help explain complex relationships
- **Summary tables**: Consolidate key information

## General Best Practices (All Report Types)

### Paragraph Structure
- **Topic sentence first**: State the main point
- **Supporting details**: Evidence, examples, data
- **Concluding sentence**: Transition or summary (optional)
- **Length**: 4-8 sentences, 100-200 words

### Transitions
- Use transitional words and phrases to connect ideas:
  - Sequence: "First", "Next", "Finally"
  - Contrast: "However", "On the other hand", "Conversely"
  - Causation: "Therefore", "Consequently", "As a result"
  - Example: "For instance", "Specifically", "In particular"

### Avoiding Common Issues
- **Avoid vague language**: "Very", "quite", "somewhat" (use specific descriptors)
- **Avoid redundancy**: "Past history", "future plans" (use "history", "plans")
- **Avoid jargon overload**: Define or explain specialized terms
- **Avoid long sentences without breaks**: Use punctuation for readability

### Revision Checklist
- [ ] Tone appropriate for report type
- [ ] Technical level matches audience
- [ ] Citations properly formatted
- [ ] Figures/tables referenced in text
- [ ] Headings descriptive and parallel
- [ ] Transitions smooth between sections
- [ ] No grammatical errors
- [ ] Consistent terminology throughout
